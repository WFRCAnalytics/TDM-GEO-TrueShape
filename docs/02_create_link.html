Redissolving candidate centerlines and splitting only at snapped model nodes to produce true-shape link pieces

CandidateTDMRoadLinks is fully re-dissolved into maximal topological chains per VERT_LEVEL (undoing incidental arcpy junction cuts; level isolation prevents GEOS from spuriously noding elevated roads against at-grade crossings), then same-route chains split by an incidental junction — a VERT_LEVEL boundary (e.g. a freeway climbing onto its own flyover) or an ordinary at-grade branch point with no TDM model node (e.g. a through-route crossed by an unrelated street not in the model) — are restitched, then the result is split at snapped WFRC road model-node positions plus CC-attachment/external-station node positions (only where resolved and not already snapped by the main pipeline, to avoid slivers) to produce the raw building-block pieces for true-shape link conflation. Lineage/display attributes (OBJECTID, UNIQUE_ID, DOT_FCLASS, VERT_LEVEL, etc.) are reattached onto each piece via a midpoint-nearest join against CandidateTDMRoadLinks — VERT_LEVEL there is informational only, not a matching key.

3 Part A — Full Network Redissolve

CandidateTDMRoadLinks is already pre-cut at arcpy-style junction points (see 00_data_preparation.qmd’s rcl_merge), including boundaries between freeway/ramp/CD rows and surface-street rows wherever they merely touch — not just at genuine multi-way branches. To make the final true-shape split points come only from snapped model nodes, every row is re-dissolved into maximal topological chains (genuine branch points, where 3+ lines share an endpoint, still remain hard boundaries after dissolve; only incidental class-boundary splits are undone).

VERT_LEVEL isolation is preserved here, but only to prevent a specific GEOS behavior: unary_union on LineStrings doesn’t just merge lines that share an endpoint — it nodes every crossing point, inserting a new vertex (and therefore a new potential break) anywhere two lines cross in the middle, even when nothing physically connects there. Per-level isolation is what stops every elevated road from being spuriously noded against every at-grade street it merely passes over. A same-level-only merge was verified against a same-network global merge: global dissolve produced more chains (9,368) than even the raw ungrouped candidate rows (7,642) — i.e. net fragmentation, not consolidation — confirming this is a real effect at scale here, not a theoretical one. Freeway rows and surface-street rows are still dissolved together within a level — only the incidental class-boundary splits are undone; genuine branch points (3+ lines sharing an endpoint) remain hard boundaries after dissolve either way.

4 Part B — Resolve Snap Coordinates

nodes_snapped.gpkg stores snapped_x_round / snapped_y_round rounded to 2 decimal places (cm precision). Splitting and M-value operations require coordinates that exactly match a vertex stored in the centerline geometry. For each snapped node, this step finds the nearest unique centerline vertex within 5 cm and records its full-precision coordinate.

FixedTransit_Rail nodes are excluded — they snap to GTFS rail infrastructure (CRT stations, LRT midpoints), not to road centerlines, so they are never road centerline split points.

5 Part B2 — Restitch Same-Route Junctions

Part A’s dissolve only auto-merges through a true degree-2 point, so it leaves two kinds of incidental split behind, both of which strand a piece boundary with no corresponding TDM model node:

  • VERT_LEVEL boundaries — where the same physical route continues across an elevation change (e.g. a freeway mainline climbing onto its own flyover), the two sides were dissolved in different level groups and so remain separate chains touching at a single point. Bridges/flyovers have no physical model node where they rise off the ground.
  • Ordinary at-grade branch points — wherever 3+ chain-ends meet, dissolve leaves the point split even if it’s really just one through-route crossed by an unrelated street that isn’t in the TDM network at all. This turns out to be the common case: e.g. HWY 186 running through as a single continuous corridor from 900 E to 1300 E is spuriously cut where 1100 E crosses it, even though 1100 E carries no TDM link and no model node sits at that crossing.

restitch_route_through_junctions finds every coordinate shared by 2+ distinct chains and merges any pair that agrees on DOT_RTNAME and POSTTYPE (same route, same facility class) there, regardless of whether the mismatch came from a VERT_LEVEL difference or an ordinary junction — and regardless of how many other, non-matching chains also touch that same point (a crossing street stays split; only the matching through-route pair merges). Points that are themselves a snapped model node are never restitched — those must stay hard boundaries.

Show the code 
gdf_fulldissolve_restitched = restitch_route_through_junctions(

6 Part C — Split at Snapped Nodes

gdf_fulldissolve_restitched (Part A + B2) — not the original CandidateTDMRoadLinks rows — is split only at snapped road model-node positions, so piece boundaries come exclusively from road-node snapping, never from incidental arcpy junction cuts or an incidental same-route junction (VERT_LEVEL boundary or unrelated at-grade crossing). Points that project to within 0.1 mm of a component’s existing endpoint are skipped (already a boundary). After all splits, each resulting piece is bounded exclusively by: - a snapped model node position, or - a genuine branch/route-identity-mismatch junction already present in gdf_fulldissolve_restitched.

CC attachment nodes (cc_nodes_snapped) and external station nodes (ext_nodes_snapped) are also used as split points here, but only the use_as_split subset — resolved within 100 m and not already snapped by the main pipeline (see Part B and 01_node_classification.qmd). Unresolved nodes are left out entirely, same as any other unsnapped node: no coordinate, no split, no change from today’s behavior for that node.

No lineage keys are carried through this split (id_cols=()) — a redissolved row no longer maps 1:1 to a single original OBJECTID. Lineage/display attributes are reattached afterward via midpoint join (Part D).

7 Part D — Build Pieces Layer, Reattach Lineage, and Export

Extract start/end endpoint coordinates for each piece (rounded to 2 dp for matching against snapped_x_round / snapped_y_round in the next stage). Since splitting now runs against the redissolved+restitched gdf_fulldissolve_restitched layer, a piece no longer maps 1:1 to a single original CandidateTDMRoadLinks OBJECTID — lineage/display attributes (TRANSFER_COLS) are reattached via the same midpoint-nearest-join approach 00_data_preparation.qmd uses to transfer attributes from raw centerlines onto CandidateTDMRoadLinks in the first place, this time from CandidateTDMRoadLinks onto each piece.

Show the code 
pieces = gdf_pieces_split.geometry.tolist()
start_coords = np.array([p.coords[0] for p in pieces])
end_coords   = np.array([p.coords[-1] for p in pieces])

gdf_pieces = gpd.GeoDataFrame(
    {
        "piece_id": np.arange(len(pieces)),
        "x_start":  np.round(start_coords[:, 0], 2),
        "y_start":  np.round(start_coords[:, 1], 2),
        "x_end":    np.round(end_coords[:, 0], 2),
        "y_end":    np.round(end_coords[:, 1], 2),
        "length_m": np.array([p.length for p in pieces]),
        "geometry": pieces,
    },
    crs=CRS_UTM,
)

VERT_LEVEL in the reattached output is informational only — it is the VERT_LEVEL of whichever CandidateTDMRoadLinks row lands nearest this piece’s midpoint, nothing more. VERT_LEVEL is used upstream only as a dissolve-grouping key (Part A, to keep GEOS from noding elevated roads against every at-grade crossing, and Part B2, to look up each chain’s own route identity within its own level) — it is never itself an identity check, a snap-coordinate key, a split key, or used in 03_transfer_attributes.qmd’s A/B chain matching (that gate is DOT_RTNAME + POSTTYPE, see Part B2). A piece can legitimately span a real elevation change or an ordinary street crossing with no split between — e.g. a freeway climbing onto a flyover, or HWY 186 running straight through where 1100 E crosses it — once Part B2 has restitched that specific junction. In that case the single VERT_LEVEL value carried onto the piece just reflects whichever side of the junction the midpoint happens to fall on; it is kept for future convenience (e.g. if the true-shape TDM link output later wants a VERT_LEVEL column) and should not be read as authoritative for the whole piece.

CandidateTDMRoadLinks contains a small number of standalone sub-centimetre “stub” features (max ~6 mm, confirmed by direct OBJECTID lookup) — digitizing artifacts, not anything this pipeline creates. Because split_candidate_links never cuts a component shorter than its 0.5 m _EPS, each stub passes through whole as its own piece; since both of its (near-identical) endpoints round to the same snapped-node coordinate, Stage 3 later resolves A_piece == B_piece for it — a spurious self-loop. Genuine physical loop roads (cloverleaf/collector loops that legitimately start and end at the same point) are tens of metres to several kilometres long, so a length_m < 1 m gate removes only the artifacts.

## Part A — Full Network Redissolve

`CandidateTDMRoadLinks` is already pre-cut at arcpy-style junction points
(see `00_data_preparation.qmd`'s `rcl_merge`), including boundaries between
freeway/ramp/CD rows and surface-street rows wherever they merely touch —
not just at genuine multi-way branches. To make the final true-shape split
points come **only** from snapped model nodes, every row is re-dissolved
into maximal topological chains (genuine branch points, where 3+ lines
share an endpoint, still remain hard boundaries after dissolve; only
incidental class-boundary splits are undone).

`VERT_LEVEL` isolation is preserved here, but only to prevent a specific
GEOS behavior: `unary_union` on LineStrings doesn't just merge lines that
share an endpoint — it **nodes every crossing point**, inserting a new
vertex (and therefore a new potential break) anywhere two lines cross in
the middle, even when nothing physically connects there. Per-level
isolation is what stops every elevated road from being spuriously noded
against every at-grade street it merely passes over. A same-level-only
merge was verified against a same-network global merge: global dissolve
produced *more* chains (9,368) than even the raw ungrouped candidate rows
(7,642) — i.e. net fragmentation, not consolidation — confirming this is
a real effect at scale here, not a theoretical one. Freeway rows and
surface-street rows are still dissolved together *within* a level — only
the incidental class-boundary splits are undone; genuine branch points (3+
lines sharing an endpoint) remain hard boundaries after dissolve either
way.

```{python}
vert_levels = sorted(gdf_centerlines["VERT_LEVEL"].dropna().unique())
print(f"VERT_LEVELs present: {vert_levels}")

fulldissolve_parts = []
for lvl in vert_levels:
    subset = gdf_centerlines[gdf_centerlines["VERT_LEVEL"] == lvl]
    if subset.empty:
        continue
    dissolved = dissolve_and_singlepart(subset)
    dissolved["dissolve_level"] = lvl
    print(f"  VERT_LEVEL {lvl}: {len(subset):,} rows -> {len(dissolved):,} dissolved chains")
    fulldissolve_parts.append(dissolved)

gdf_fulldissolve = gpd.GeoDataFrame(
    pd.concat(fulldissolve_parts, ignore_index=True),
    geometry="geometry",
    crs=gdf_centerlines.crs,
)
print(f"\nTotal: {len(gdf_centerlines):,} candidate rows -> {len(gdf_fulldissolve):,} redissolved chains")
```

Sanity check: dissolve only merges touching lines into longer chains — it
never adds or removes length. Total length before and after must match.

```{python}
len_before = gdf_centerlines.geometry.length.sum()
len_after = gdf_fulldissolve.geometry.length.sum()
diff_m = abs(len_before - len_after)
print(f"Total length before: {len_before:,.1f} m")
print(f"Total length after:  {len_after:,.1f} m")
print(f"Difference:          {diff_m:.3f} m")
assert diff_m < 1.0, "Redissolve changed total network length — investigate before proceeding."
```

## Part B — Resolve Snap Coordinates

`nodes_snapped.gpkg` stores `snapped_x_round / snapped_y_round` rounded to 2 decimal
places (cm precision). Splitting and M-value operations require coordinates that
exactly match a vertex stored in the centerline geometry. For each snapped node,
this step finds the nearest unique centerline vertex within 5 cm and records its
full-precision coordinate.

FixedTransit_Rail nodes are excluded — they snap to GTFS rail infrastructure
(CRT stations, LRT midpoints), not to road centerlines, so they are never road
centerline split points.

## Part B2 — Restitch Same-Route Junctions

Part A's dissolve only auto-merges through a true degree-2 point, so it
leaves two kinds of incidental split behind, both of which strand a piece
boundary with no corresponding TDM model node:

- **VERT_LEVEL boundaries** — where the *same* physical route continues
  across an elevation change (e.g. a freeway mainline climbing onto its own
  flyover), the two sides were dissolved in different level groups and so
  remain separate chains touching at a single point. Bridges/flyovers have
  no physical model node where they rise off the ground.
- **Ordinary at-grade branch points** — wherever 3+ chain-ends meet,
  dissolve leaves the point split even if it's really just one through-route
  crossed by an unrelated street that isn't in the TDM network at all. This
  turns out to be the common case: e.g. HWY 186 running through as a single
  continuous corridor from 900 E to 1300 E is spuriously cut where 1100 E
  crosses it, even though 1100 E carries no TDM link and no model node sits
  at that crossing.

`restitch_route_through_junctions` finds every coordinate shared by 2+
distinct chains and merges any pair that agrees on `DOT_RTNAME` and
`POSTTYPE` (same route, same facility class) there, regardless of whether
the mismatch came from a VERT_LEVEL difference or an ordinary junction —
and regardless of how many other, non-matching chains also touch that same
point (a crossing street stays split; only the matching through-route pair
merges). Points that are themselves a snapped model node are never
restitched — those must stay hard boundaries.

```{python}
gdf_fulldissolve_restitched = restitch_route_through_junctions(
    gdf_fulldissolve,
    "dissolve_level",
    gdf_centerlines,
    "VERT_LEVEL",
    identity_cols=("DOT_RTNAME", "POSTTYPE"),
    exclude_points=snap_points_exact,
    point_tolerance=0.05,
)
attrs = gdf_fulldissolve_restitched.attrs
print(f"Junction points found (2+ chains touching): {attrs['n_candidate_points']:,}")
print(f"  excluded (at a snapped node):          {attrs['n_excluded']:,}")
print(f"  candidate chain pairs considered:      {attrs['n_candidate_pairs']:,}")
print(f"  route-identity mismatch (left split):  {attrs['n_identity_mismatch']:,}")
print(f"  restitched (same route, merged):       {attrs['n_merged']:,} edges -> {attrs['n_components_merged']:,} chains")
print(f"Chains: {len(gdf_fulldissolve):,} -> {len(gdf_fulldissolve_restitched):,}")
```


## Part C — Split at Snapped Nodes

`gdf_fulldissolve_restitched` (Part A + B2) — not the original
`CandidateTDMRoadLinks` rows — is split only at snapped road model-node
positions, so piece boundaries come exclusively from road-node snapping,
never from incidental arcpy junction cuts or an incidental same-route
junction (VERT_LEVEL boundary or unrelated at-grade crossing). Points that
project to within 0.1 mm of a component's existing endpoint are skipped
(already a boundary). After all splits, each resulting piece is bounded
exclusively by:
- a snapped model node position, or
- a genuine branch/route-identity-mismatch junction already present in
  `gdf_fulldissolve_restitched`.

CC attachment nodes (`cc_nodes_snapped`) and external station nodes
(`ext_nodes_snapped`) are also used as split points here, but only the
`use_as_split` subset — resolved within 100 m and not already snapped by the
main pipeline (see Part B and `01_node_classification.qmd`). Unresolved
nodes are left out entirely, same as any other unsnapped node: no coordinate,
no split, no change from today's behavior for that node.

No lineage keys are carried through this split (`id_cols=()`) — a redissolved
row no longer maps 1:1 to a single original `OBJECTID`. Lineage/display
attributes are reattached afterward via midpoint join (Part D).

```{python}
all_snap_points = snap_points_exact + cc_points_exact + ext_points_exact
```

## Part D — Build Pieces Layer, Reattach Lineage, and Export

Extract start/end endpoint coordinates for each piece (rounded to 2 dp for
matching against `snapped_x_round / snapped_y_round` in the next stage). Since
splitting now runs against the redissolved+restitched
`gdf_fulldissolve_restitched` layer, a piece no longer maps 1:1 to a single
original `CandidateTDMRoadLinks` `OBJECTID` —
lineage/display attributes (`TRANSFER_COLS`) are reattached via the same
midpoint-nearest-join approach `00_data_preparation.qmd` uses to transfer
attributes from raw centerlines onto `CandidateTDMRoadLinks` in the first
place, this time from `CandidateTDMRoadLinks` onto each piece.

```{python}
pieces = gdf_pieces_split.geometry.tolist()
start_coords = np.array([p.coords[0] for p in pieces])
end_coords   = np.array([p.coords[-1] for p in pieces])

gdf_pieces = gpd.GeoDataFrame(
    {
        "piece_id": np.arange(len(pieces)),
        "x_start":  np.round(start_coords[:, 0], 2),
        "y_start":  np.round(start_coords[:, 1], 2),
        "x_end":    np.round(end_coords[:, 0], 2),
        "y_end":    np.round(end_coords[:, 1], 2),
        "length_m": np.array([p.length for p in pieces]),
        "geometry": pieces,
    },
    crs=CRS_UTM,
)
```

`VERT_LEVEL` in the reattached output is informational only — it is the
`VERT_LEVEL` of whichever `CandidateTDMRoadLinks` row lands nearest this
piece's midpoint, nothing more. `VERT_LEVEL` is used upstream only as a
dissolve-grouping key (Part A, to keep GEOS from noding elevated roads
against every at-grade crossing, and Part B2, to look up each chain's own
route identity within its own level) — it is never itself an identity
check, a snap-coordinate key, a split key, or used in
`03_transfer_attributes.qmd`'s A/B chain matching (that gate is
`DOT_RTNAME` + `POSTTYPE`, see Part B2). A piece can legitimately span a
real elevation change or an ordinary street crossing with no split between
— e.g. a freeway climbing onto a flyover, or HWY 186 running straight
through where 1100 E crosses it — once Part B2 has restitched that
specific junction. In that case the single `VERT_LEVEL` value carried onto
the piece just reflects whichever side of the junction the midpoint
happens to fall on; it is kept for future convenience (e.g. if the
true-shape TDM link output later wants a `VERT_LEVEL` column) and should
not be read as authoritative for the whole piece.

`CandidateTDMRoadLinks` contains a small number of standalone sub-centimetre
"stub" features (max ~6 mm, confirmed by direct `OBJECTID` lookup) — digitizing
artifacts, not anything this pipeline creates. Because `split_candidate_links`
never cuts a component shorter than its 0.5 m `_EPS`, each stub passes through
whole as its own piece; since both of its (near-identical) endpoints round to
the same snapped-node coordinate, Stage 3 later resolves `A_piece == B_piece`
for it — a spurious self-loop. Genuine physical loop roads (cloverleaf/collector
loops that legitimately start and end at the same point) are tens of metres to
several kilometres long, so a `length_m < 1 m` gate removes only the artifacts.

```{python}
n_before_drop = len(gdf_pieces)
degenerate_mask = (
    (gdf_pieces["x_start"] == gdf_pieces["x_end"])
    & (gdf_pieces["y_start"] == gdf_pieces["y_end"])
    & (gdf_pieces["length_m"] < 1.0)
)
n_degenerate = int(degenerate_mask.sum())
gdf_pieces = gdf_pieces[~degenerate_mask].reset_index(drop=True)
print(
    f"Dropped {n_degenerate:,} / {n_before_drop:,} degenerate zero-length pieces "
    f"(start == end, length < 1 m) — pre-existing near-duplicate-vertex stub "
    f"features already present as standalone rows in CandidateTDMRoadLinks, "
    f"not created by this pipeline. Real closed-loop roads (cloverleaf/collector "
    f"loops, tens of metres to several km long) are left untouched — the length "
    f"gate cleanly separates the two populations with a >3,000x margin."
)

gdf_pieces = transfer_attributes_by_midpoint(gdf_pieces, gdf_centerlines, TRANSFER_COLS)

n_null = gdf_pieces[TRANSFER_COLS].isna().any(axis=1).sum()
print(f"Pieces with a null transferred attribute: {n_null:,} / {len(gdf_pieces):,}")

PATHS["out_link_pieces"].parent.mkdir(parents=True, exist_ok=True)
gdf_pieces.to_file(PATHS["out_link_pieces"], driver="GPKG")
print(f"Exported {len(gdf_pieces):,} pieces → {PATHS['out_link_pieces']}")
```
